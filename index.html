4. Future directions

In this proof-of-concept evaluation, projecting cluster-level expression onto a schematic embryo reproduced observed ISH patterns in of scored gene–stage pairs (mean Jaccard index ). Agreement was highest for genes whose expression is confined to a single tissue that Daniocell resolves as a discrete cluster, including cdh17 in the pronephros, gata1a in the erythroid lineage, mpx in myeloid cells and tbxta in the notochord. Discordant and partially concordant pairs fell into two categories that call for different remedies.

The first category reflects limited anatomical resolution. In scored pairs, the ISH annotation named a structure finer than any Daniocell tissue group, such as the midbrain–hindbrain boundary, individual rhombomeres, the epiphysis or the optic stalk. Because the current projection assigns each broad group to a single region, spatially restricted neural genes such as pax2a and egr2b are diluted within the large "neural" group. A first revision will therefore project Daniocell sub-clusters onto correspondingly finer cartoon regions. A preliminary inspection supports this approach: egr2b expression is strong in the sub-cluster annotated as midbrain and hindbrain progenitors (neur.6) but falls below the calling threshold once that sub-cluster is merged into the broader neural group.

The second category reflects limited transcript detection. In scored pairs, ISH detected expression in a region where the corresponding Daniocell groups showed little or no signal, as for crx and for egr2b before 48 hpf. Finer spatial mapping cannot recover transcripts that were not captured during single-cell profiling, so these discrepancies are better addressed by comparison with deeper or targeted datasets, or by experimental validation. Similarly, apparent expression arising from ambient RNA, as observed for krt4, may be reduced by applying ambient-RNA correction before projection.

More computationally intensive strategies may be warranted for structures that remain unresolved after sub-cluster projection. Landmark-based spatial inference, in which cells are assigned positions using a panel of genes with known ISH patterns (as in the original Seurat framework; Satija et al., 2015), could resolve neural domains along the anterior–posterior axis, although it requires a curated landmark panel for each developmental stage. Integration with spatial transcriptomic references using mapping methods such as Tangram (Biancalani et al., 2021) or novoSpaRc (Nitzan et al., 2019) offers the highest potential resolution but is limited to the stages and tissue sections those references cover. We will evaluate these approaches only for regions in which sub-cluster projection fails to improve agreement. Future versions will also expand the gene set, add intermediate developmental stages and replace estimates read from published plots with values computed directly from Daniocell count data.

5. Methods

Data sources

Single-cell expression data were obtained from Daniocell (Sur et al., 2023), a whole-embryo single-cell RNA-sequencing atlas of zebrafish development. For each gene, the fraction of cells expressing the transcript and the mean expression level were extracted for every tissue group from the gene's published dot plot, in which dot size encodes the percentage of expressing cells and dot color encodes mean expression. Mean expression was rescaled from 0 to 1 relative to the maximum of each gene's own color scale. Because these values were estimated from dot color rather than computed from count matrices, they should be treated as approximate. Observed expression patterns were obtained from the Zebrafish Information Network (ZFIN; wild-type expression data, release of 19 November 2026), restricted to annotations from the Thisse high-throughput whole-mount ISH screens (ZFIN publications ZDB-PUB-010810-1, ZDB-PUB-040907-1, ZDB-PUB-051025-1 and ZDB-PUB-080227-22). Figure identifiers linking each annotation to its original ISH images were retrieved from the corresponding ZFIN expression, figure and image tables (release of 21 November 2026).

Projection of single-cell expression onto embryonic anatomy

Each of the 43 Daniocell tissue groups was assigned to one of 21 regions of a schematic lateral-view embryo, and each ZFIN anatomy term was assigned to the same set of regions. Groups without a clear anatomical counterpart (blastomeres, primordial germ cells and taste cells) were left unmapped and flagged when they carried signal. A region was classified as expressing a gene when at least one of its assigned groups contained ≥ % expressing cells with a relative mean expression of ≥ . Weaker signal (≥ 10% of cells and ≥ 0.15 relative expression) was displayed with reduced intensity but excluded from scoring. Display intensity for each region was calculated as relative mean expression multiplied by the fraction of expressing cells, with the fraction saturating at 60%. The complete mapping tables are provided below.

Developmental stage matching

Three stage windows were compared: segmentation (Daniocell 14–21 hpf; Thisse 14–19 somites), pharyngula (24–34 hpf; 20–25 somites to Prim-25) and hatching (48–58 hpf; High-pec to Long-pec). The hatching ISH window (approximately 42–48 hpf) precedes the corresponding Daniocell window, and a single schematic body plan was used for all three stages.

Agreement scoring

For each gene–stage pair, agreement between predicted and observed regions was quantified using the Jaccard index, defined as the number of regions identified by both sources divided by the number identified by either. Pairs were classified as concordant (J ≥ 0.67), partially concordant (0.25 ≤ J < 0.67) or discordant (J < 0.25). Pairs lacking a Thisse annotation were not scored, because absence of an annotation does not establish absence of expression. Each discrepancy was further classified by probable cause: anatomical resolution finer than the available clusters, signal below the calling threshold, no detection in the corresponding clusters, predicted expression without ISH support, or signal in an unmapped group.

Limitations

Single-cell measurements are subject to transcript dropout, loss or under-representation of fragile cell types during dissociation, and contamination by ambient RNA, the last of which likely explains the low-level krt4 signal detected across nearly all groups. ISH annotations from high-throughput screens can be incomplete; for example, pax2a expression in the pronephros is not annotated at 14–19 somites. The schematic embryo is not drawn to scale and does not represent stage-specific morphology. ISHimulation is intended for research and hypothesis generation, and simulated patterns should be confirmed against the original ISH images before interpretation.
